## 1. Purpose

This specification is written **before selecting any complex-number carrier, representation, identity regime, or arithmetic implementation**.

It defines what BOMA must mean by an accepted Stage-One complex domain independently of whether a later construction uses:

```text
ordered pairs / Cartesian coordinates
formal expressions a + b·i
polynomial quotients such as R[X]/(X²+1)
a rank-two algebra presentation
another algebraically equivalent realization
```

No such realization is made canonical by this document.

The governing separation is:

```text
acceptance semantics
≠
selected producer route
≠
representation identity
≠
Lean packaging
```

The specification must therefore constrain the **exported mathematical interface** strongly enough to identify the intended complex extension of accepted `R`, while leaving its construction branchable.

# 2. Ex-ante semantic decision

The Stage-One complex domain is required to be a **quadratic field extension of the accepted real domain generated by a distinguished square root of `-1`**, with a unique real/imaginary decomposition.

At interface level this means that an accepted candidate must provide:

```text
an explicit carrier C_BOMA and explicit identity;
a faithful embedding ιR : R_BOMA → C_BOMA;
a commutative-field-strength algebraic interface;
a distinguished element I : C_BOMA;
I * I = -1;
for every z : C_BOMA, real coefficients a,b exist with
  z = ιR(a) + ιR(b) * I;
and those coefficients are unique.
```

The expression above is a theorem/interface statement. It does **not** declare that the carrier is `R × R`, nor that elements are definitionally stored as two coordinates.

The uniqueness clause is essential to prevent an arbitrary larger field containing a square root of `-1` from satisfying the contract merely by containing a copy of the intended complex field.

## 2.1 Why field structure alone is insufficient

A field extending `R` is not enough to identify the complex domain. In particular, the accepted real field itself already supplies field structure and an identity embedding into an extension target of the same carrier.

Therefore:

```text
FIELD EXTENSION ONLY = REJECTED AS A C ACCEPTANCE CONTRACT
```

## 2.2 Why a square root of `-1` alone is insufficient

A field extension containing an element `I` with `I² = -1` can still be strictly larger than the intended complex extension.

Therefore:

```text
FIELD + I²=-1 ONLY = REJECTED AS A COMPLETE C ACCEPTANCE CONTRACT
```

The generation and coordinate-uniqueness clauses are required to exclude such over-large realizations at the declared Stage-One scope.

## 2.3 Algebraic closure scope

Full algebraic closure is **not** a Stage-One acceptance obligation in this specification.

Reason:

1. the quadratic generated-extension contract already characterizes the intended `R(i)` interface up to the appropriate `R`-algebra isomorphism preserving `I`;
2. algebraic closure is a substantial further theorem about this field, not a carrier-selection principle required to distinguish the intended quadratic extension from competing representations;
3. proving it would introduce a separate polynomial/root-existence/FTA-scale dependency program and could silently demand real-analysis or real-closedness infrastructure not present in the accepted `R-BLOCK-001` export;
4. such a theorem may be constructed later through its own PDSA lineage and then promoted as a strengthening only after its dependencies are explicit.

Thus BOMA must not state:

```text
C_BOMA is algebraically closed
```

until that property is independently constructed and verified.

This is a **scope decision about Stage-One acceptance**, not a claim that algebraic closure is mathematically unimportant.

# 4. Stage-One C acceptance obligations

## CA-01 — Explicit complex carrier

Construct an explicit carrier:

```text
C_BOMA
```

from accepted earlier material and declared new C-stage infrastructure.

A built-in or standard-library complex carrier may not be adopted by declaration as the BOMA construction.

## CA-02 — Explicit complex identity

State exactly when two constructed complex representatives count as the same `C_BOMA` value.

Possible later regimes include definitional equality on a canonical carrier, an explicit equivalence relation, normalization, or quotient identity. The selected identity must be visible as a representation/formalization commitment where applicable.

## CA-03 — Faithful real embedding

Construct:

```text
ιR : R_BOMA → C_BOMA
```

and prove injectivity.

The statement is acceptance-required; its implementation is representation-dependent.

## CA-04 — Complex algebra / field behavior

Construct or derive on the accepted carrier:

```text
0_C
1_C
negation
addition
multiplication
nonzero multiplicative inverse interface
```

and prove nontrivial commutative-field-strength behavior.

As in accepted R, a global Choice-backed inverse selector is **not automatically required**. Existence plus uniqueness witnesses are admissible if they supply the required field interface.

A particular formula for multiplication or reciprocal is not acceptance-required.

## CA-05 — Real embedding preserves field structure

Prove that `ιR` preserves the accepted R structure required by C:

```text
0
1
negation
addition
multiplication
```

and reflects equality by injectivity.

No order on C is required by this clause.

## CA-06 — Distinguished imaginary unit

Construct or identify a distinguished element:

```text
I : C_BOMA
```

at the accepted carrier/equality level.

The fact that a selected representation stores `I` as a particular coordinate or polynomial class, if it does, is route-specific.

## CA-07 — Imaginary-unit relation

Prove:

```text
I * I = -1_C
```

where `-1_C` is the image/field-theoretic negative of the complex unit.

This relation is part of the acceptance semantics, not a representation slogan.

## CA-08 — Real/imaginary generation

Prove:

```text
∀ z : C_BOMA,
  ∃ a b : R_BOMA,
    z = ιR(a) + ιR(b) * I.
```

This is a generation theorem. It does not require `C_BOMA` to be definitionally a Cartesian product.

## CA-09 — Coordinate uniqueness

Prove that the coefficients in `CA-08` are unique:

```text
ιR(a) + ιR(b) * I = ιR(a') + ιR(b') * I
  →
a = a' ∧ b = b'.
```

Together, `CA-08` and `CA-09` express that `{1,I}` is a real basis at the intended acceptance granularity without selecting a carrier representation.

## CA-10 — Non-real-collapse consequence

Certify that the imaginary generator does not collapse into the embedded real line. A preferred consequence is:

```text
¬ ∃ r : R_BOMA, I = ιR(r).
```

This need not be primitive. It may be derived from `CA-07`, `CA-09`, and/or the accepted ordered-field properties of R.

If the formal proof needs the real theorem that `-1` is not a square, that theorem must be proved downstream from the accepted R interface or exposed as an explicit dependency question; the Dedekind representation may not be used as a shortcut.

## CA-11 — Quadratic-extension comparison adequacy

The final accepted package must make the representation-neutral comparison principle available at least at the following mathematical strength:

> Any two candidate C realizations satisfying the same accepted R embedding, field laws, distinguished-`I` relation, and unique real/imaginary decomposition admit the canonical coordinate-preserving `R`-field/algebra isomorphism sending one distinguished `I` to the other.

This obligation may be discharged directly, or through a stronger explicitly stated theorem.

It is the main route-neutral reason that independently constructed candidate representations can later reconverge without being definitionally identical.

## CA-12 — Representation adequacy

For the selected Stage-One producer, state exactly what the representation classifies and which part of `CA-01..11` is mathematical interface versus route-specific realization.

Do not claim a universal property stronger than what has actually been constructed.

# 5. Necessity classification at specification time

The following distinctions govern all later C work.

## Acceptance-required mathematical content

```text
explicit carrier and identity
faithful R embedding
commutative-field-strength behavior
structure-preserving R embedding
distinguished I
I² = -1
R + R·I generation
uniqueness of real/imaginary coordinates
non-collapse of I from the real line
route-neutral comparison adequacy
final verified integration
```

These are necessary **relative to `BOMA-C-ACCEPT-001`**, not metaphysical claims that every mathematical presentation of complex numbers must use the same vocabulary.

## Not mathematically forced by the specification

```text
carrier = R × R
carrier = a quotient
carrier = polynomial classes
literal a+bi syntax
matrix representation
coordinate storage order
specific multiplication formula
specific inverse formula
specific proof of -1 nonsquare in R
specific logical proof principle
Lean structure/record/Quotient packaging
```

## Explicit Stage-One non-requirements

```text
full algebraic closure / Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
complex analytic completeness or metric topology
transcendental functions
polar/exponential representation
choice of a total order on C
identification with a standard-library Complex type
```

Any of these may become a later strengthening only through explicit construction and acceptance lineage.
