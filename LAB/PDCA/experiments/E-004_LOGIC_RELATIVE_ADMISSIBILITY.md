# E-004 — Logic-Relative Admissibility

**Cycle:** `PDCA-001`  
**Status:** Experimental / provisional  
**Source record:** `LAB/LOGIC_RELATIVE_ADMISSIBILITY_PROBE_001.md`  
**Epistemic status:** `HYPOTHESIS`

## Question

Does Brick admissibility, and the assessment of apparent conflict, depend on the logical regime in which the commitments are evaluated?

## Trigger

The preceding conflict/compatibility probe showed that dependency and compatibility are distinct. A further question arose: can compatibility itself be assessed independently of the logical framework?

The probe therefore distinguishes:

```text
contradiction
logical consequence
logical strengthening
unresolved status
```

rather than treating every difference in inferential strength as conflict.

## Logical regimes under comparison

### L-I — Intuitionistic baseline

The default regime of this probe. In general it does not validate classical principles such as:

```text
P ∨ ¬P
¬¬P → P
```

### L-C — Classical extension

A regime in which classical principles such as excluded middle and double-negation elimination are available.

### L-I+C — Intuitionistic base plus explicit classical commitment

A transition case in which the constructive framework remains visible while an additional classical principle is explicitly admitted.

This is a probe taxonomy, not a final BOMA taxonomy.

## Candidate commitments

```text
B1: ¬¬P → P
B2: P ∨ ¬P
B3: P
B4: ¬P
```

The test asks whether each is:

1. admissible in the regime;
2. a strengthening rather than a contradiction;
3. contradictory with existing commitments;
4. unresolved within the regime.

## Observations

### B1 — Double-negation elimination

`¬¬P → P` is not generally derivable in pure intuitionistic logic. Therefore adding it to an intuitionistic context should not automatically be classified as inconsistent.

A more precise provisional description is an **additional classical-strength commitment** whose introduction is architecturally visible.

In a classical regime, the same proposition is already available as part of the regime and therefore does not represent the same extension event.

### B2 — Excluded middle

Likewise, `P ∨ ¬P` is not generally derivable intuitionistically. Adding it is therefore not automatically a contradiction; it is a logical strengthening / classical commitment.

### B3 and B4

`P` and `¬P` are individually admissible commitments. Their joint status depends on the semantics of the context. Under the ordinary reading, imposing both produces genuine incompatibility.

The conflict is generated by the joint commitments, not by independence and not merely by choosing intuitionistic logic.

## Provisional classification

| Candidate | L-I | L-C | Architectural interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `¬¬P → P` | Not generally derivable | Available | Classical-strengthening commitment in L-I; ordinary consequence in L-C |
| `P ∨ ¬P` | Not generally derivable | Available | Classical-strengthening commitment in L-I; ordinary consequence in L-C |
| `P` | Admissible | Admissible | Ordinary commitment |
| `¬P` | Admissible | Admissible | Ordinary commitment |
| `P + ¬P` | Incompatible under standard reading | Incompatible under standard reading | Genuine conflict |

## Key finding

The probe supports:

```text
logical non-derivability
        ≠
contradiction
```

and:

```text
classical strengthening
        ≠
logical inconsistency
```

A candidate Brick should therefore not be rejected merely because its commitment is not derivable in the current logical regime.

Instead, the status should be classified more carefully:

```text
1. already derivable
2. admissible but not derivable
3. requires explicit logical strengthening
4. contradictory with context
5. unresolved / requires further analysis
```

## Consequence for the trunk policy

The probe strengthens the usefulness of an intuitionistic-default trunk policy, but does not by itself establish the final policy.

A classical commitment introduced into an intuitionistic context should be distinguishable from a contradiction. It may be legitimate in an explicitly strengthened branch, while trunk introduction requires the stronger necessity, justification, and transparency conditions already under consideration.

Therefore:

```text
classical ≠ invalid
non-intuitionistic ≠ contradiction
not intuitionistically provable ≠ conflict
```

The trunk question is consequently about **logical commitment and necessity**, not merely consistency.

## Candidate context-sensitive model

The probe suggests:

```text
Admissible(B, Context, LogicRegime)
```

where `Context` contains established commitments and `LogicRegime` identifies the active logical framework.

This remains a research model only.

## Relation to E-003

`E-003` established the need to distinguish dependency from compatibility. `E-004` adds a further qualification: compatibility/admissibility may itself be relative to the logical regime.

Thus the working chain is:

```text
Dependency
   ≠ Compatibility
         ≠ Admissibility
               ↓
       Logic-relative context
```

These relations should not yet be collapsed into one predicate.

## Limitations

This record preserves the source probe's conceptual analysis. It is not a formal Lean/Coq/Agda/Isabelle verification of the complete admissibility model.

In particular, the experiment does not settle whether logical regime should be defined proof-theoretically, semantically, as an explicit framework configuration, or through a combination of these.

## Open questions

1. What exactly counts as a logical regime in BOMA?
2. Should a Brick explicitly declare the logical principles it requires?
3. How should a classical-strengthening Brick be represented in an intuitionistic branch?
4. Can local admissibility be checked without global consistency computation?
5. How should conservative extensions be distinguished from genuinely stronger logical commitments?
6. Should non-conflict be defined proof-theoretically, semantically, or both?
