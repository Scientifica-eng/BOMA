# S-001 — Provisional Pre-Numerical Starting Point

**Cycle:** `PDCA-001`  
**Status:** Provisional / experimental  
**Purpose:** Record the current choice of starting point before constructing the first mathematical Brick.

## Decision

We will investigate the pre-numerical foundation from the following starting perspective:

```text
context of construction
        ↓
minimal distinguishability
        ↓
possible distinguished entity
```

This is a **constructional hypothesis**, not a claim about the metaphysical beginning of mathematics and not a claim that philosophy of mathematics has established this as the unique starting point.

The active logical baseline is **intuitionistic logic**.

## Why this starting point

The project goal is to construct the natural numbers from the weakest plausible pre-numerical structure rather than importing natural-number structure under another name.

The proposed starting point therefore avoids taking the following as primitive:

- natural numbers;
- Peano axioms;
- set-theoretic membership as the starting ontology;
- classical excluded middle;
- a completed mathematical universe of objects.

The motivation is informed by constructive/intuitionistic and structural perspectives, but the resulting construction is a BOMA experiment, not a historical reconstruction of Brouwer, Heyting, Dedekind, Peano, or any other author.

## Critical qualification: context

The word `context` is potentially dangerous.

If context is defined as a set of objects, a container of objects, or a pre-existing relational universe, then substantial structure may already have been smuggled into the foundation.

Therefore, at this stage:

```text
Context ≠ assumed mathematical set
Context ≠ assumed collection of objects
Context ≠ assumed completed universe
```

Instead, `context` is provisionally understood as the **conditions under which a construction or distinction can be specified**. Its exact formal content remains an open question.

## Critical qualification: object

We deliberately do **not** make “first object” the first primitive.

A stronger candidate for the first construction is a minimal act/relation of distinguishability.

The working question is:

> What is the least intuitionistically acceptable structure that permits a distinction without presupposing number?

Only after answering that question should we decide whether a distinguished entity/object must be introduced, and what “object” means in this setting.

## Philosophical sources of inspiration

The choice is informed by several traditions:

### Constructive / intuitionistic mathematics

Mathematical existence is treated constructively rather than as unrestricted assertion of existence in a completed mathematical universe. This supports investigating construction before treating objects as given.

### Structuralism

Mathematical identity can be understood through structural position and relations rather than through an assumed intrinsic nature of mathematical objects. This motivates testing whether distinction/relation should precede object-language.

### Dedekind / structural approaches to the natural numbers

The natural numbers can be approached through structural organization rather than treating individual numbers as unexplained primitives. This is relevant as a later comparison point, not as a commitment to Dedekind's construction.

### Peano-style axiomatization

The Peano framework will be treated primarily as a **test of the resulting natural-number construction**, rather than as our initial source of the natural-number concepts.

### Type-theoretic / constructive foundations

The idea that a context of construction may be more primitive than a pre-existing universe of sets is relevant as methodological inspiration. We do not import a particular type theory as BOMA's foundation at this stage.

## Current candidate construction path

The following is a hypothesis, not a committed roadmap:

```text
conditions of construction
        ↓
minimal distinction
        ↓
identity / repeatability
        ↓
configuration or collection
        ↓
iteration / succession
        ↓
natural-number structure
        ↓
ℕ
```

Each arrow must be earned by an actual construction or argument. None is accepted merely because it is philosophically attractive.

## Alternatives considered

### A. Set-first

Start with sets and membership.

**Concern:** may introduce too much structure before the pre-numerical foundation is understood.

### B. Peano-first

Start with `0`, successor, and induction.

**Concern:** starts too close to the target and risks hiding the pre-numerical work we explicitly want to expose.

### C. Dedekind/structural-first

Start from a structural conception related to chains/systems.

**Assessment:** relevant and potentially useful, but requires clarification of the primitive structure and may already encode substantial order/iteration concepts.

### D. Context + distinguishability

Start from conditions of construction and investigate minimal distinguishability.

**Current assessment:** most promising for the stated experimental goal, but explicitly provisional.

## Failure tests

The approach must be reconsidered if any of the following occurs:

1. Defining `context` requires a stronger ontology than the project intends to permit.
2. Defining `distinction` already presupposes the identity notion we intended to derive.
3. A minimal distinction cannot be formalized coherently under the intuitionistic baseline.
4. The route to natural numbers requires an additional foundational commitment that was not declared.
5. The construction becomes merely a disguised restatement of an existing natural-number structure.

Failure is an acceptable experimental result. A failed starting point must be documented rather than silently replaced.

## First construction target

Before creating a Brick for a “first object”, we will attempt to construct and formalize:

```text
B-001 candidate:
Minimal Distinction
```

The Brick is not yet accepted. Its acceptance depends on the actual construction attempt.

## Calibration safeguard

This document records a choice for experimentation. It must not later be cited as evidence that the chosen starting point is mathematically necessary, historically canonical, or philosophically proven superior.

The construction itself is the decisive test.
