# EXP-003 — Minimum Specification Act

**Brick / context:** `B-001`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Determine whether a minimal act of specification/distinction can be stated without importing object identity, multiplicity, or number.

## 2. Experiment

Test the weakest operational reading of “specification”: a construction may single out a result as the result of that construction, without first declaring a universe of objects or a binary relation between pre-existing relata.

We distinguish three notions:

- **construction:** an act/process whose outcome is available;
- **result:** whatever is obtained by that construction;
- **recognition:** the ability to identify the same result when the construction is repeated or referred to again.

No claim is made that these are yet formal primitives.

## 3. Result

The attempt reveals that “result” alone is insufficient for stable mathematical use: to refer to a result again, we need at least some notion of repeatability or recognition. However, introducing full equality immediately would be stronger than necessary and risks importing an already developed identity theory.

Thus the construction seems to require a distinction between:

```text
obtaining a result
```

and

```text
being able to recognize/refer to that result again
```

This is a narrower dependency than the binary `D(a,b)` formulation, but it still shows that a usable pre-numerical object cannot be obtained from a one-off act alone.

## 4. Decision

Do not yet introduce an equality relation as primitive. Retain **construction + repeatable recognition** as the current candidate minimal structure, subject to formalization.

The working hypothesis is that a first usable entity may arise as the stable/re-identifiable result of a construction, rather than as an unexplained object given in advance.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Provisional`

## 6. Next Cycle

Test whether repeatable recognition can be specified without presupposing numerical repetition or full equality, and determine whether this yields the first acceptable distinguishable entity.
