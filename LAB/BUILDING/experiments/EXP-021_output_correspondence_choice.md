# EXP-021 — Explicit Output Correspondence Choice

**Brick / context:** `B-001`, `B-002`, `EXP-020`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test the weakest explicit rule that could connect separately recorded occurrences of the distinction-making act with separately referable outputs.

## 2. Experiment

Introduce the following constructional convention:

> Each separately recorded occurrence of `δ` is assigned its own referable output token.

Thus:

```text
δ₁ → r₁
δ₂ → r₂
```

The subscripts record the constructional provenance of the outputs. The convention is an explicit choice; it is not derived from the previous Bricks.

## 3. Result

The convention succeeds in producing two separately referable output tokens in the construction history. It therefore gives us a controlled form of pre-numerical multiplicity:

```text
r₁     r₂
│       │
δ₁      δ₂
```

However, the rule does not by itself assert a general equality/inequality theory. We have stipulated that different recorded occurrences receive different output tokens; the meaning of “different” here is tied to provenance in the construction history.

This is the first point at which the path deliberately chooses a rule capable of generating more than one referable constructional token.

## 4. Decision

Accept the correspondence convention as a new explicit constructional choice and create a new Brick representing **provenance-indexed referable multiplicity**.

Do not identify this multiplicity with the number two. Do not yet form a set, cardinal, pair, or natural number.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Provisional / chosen rule`

## 6. Next Cycle

Construct and document the corresponding Brick. Then test what operations become possible on two provenance-distinguished referable tokens without importing numerical language.
