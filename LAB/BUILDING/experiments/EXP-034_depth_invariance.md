# EXP-034 — Depth Invariance of Constructional Stages

**Context:** `B-012`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether constructional depth can be treated as a structural feature independent of the particular referable tokens occurring in a stage, and whether different stages at the same depth must be identified.

## 2. Experiment

Consider two possible finite construction histories of equal length:

```text
s₀ → s₁ → s₂

s₀ → t₁ → t₂
```

where the intermediate and final referable stage-results may differ in their internal provenance or tokens, but each history contains two extension occurrences after the initial stage.

Compare this with histories of different lengths:

```text
s₀ → s₁

s₀ → t₁ → t₂
```

Ask whether equal depth forces identity, and whether unequal depth forces distinction.

## 3. Result

The construction does not force identity of equal-depth stages. Two histories can have the same extension depth while remaining distinct constructional results if their provenance or internal construction differs.

Likewise, unequal depths are structurally distinguishable by the number of extension occurrences in the recorded histories.

Therefore depth behaves as a **structural invariant of a construction history**, but it is not identical with the full identity of the stage.

This suggests a new distinction:

- **stage identity:** the particular constructional result, including its provenance;
- **depth class:** the abstraction obtained by retaining only the number of extension steps from the initial stage.

The second notion is much closer to a candidate pre-numerical “number-like” object.

However, saying that two histories have the same finite length still uses an informal notion of finite counting. The experiment therefore does not yet produce a fully independent number object. It reveals the structural operation that such an object would have to capture.

## 4. Decision

Accept depth as a candidate invariant, but do **not** identify depth classes with natural numbers yet.

Record the possibility of constructing an abstraction that forgets token/provenance details while preserving extension depth.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Provisional structural insight`

## 6. Next Cycle

Test whether the abstraction by depth can be represented as equivalence classes of finite construction histories, and whether extension acts consistently on those classes. This will determine whether a genuine pre-numerical quotient structure can be built before assigning numerical names.
