# EXP-008 — Constructional Role Distinguishability

**Brick / context:** `B-001`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether one constructional role can be distinguished from another without presupposing numerical multiplicity or the concept of “two”.

## 2. Experiment

Consider two constructional specifications/roles only through their admissible behavior: if a role permits a constructional outcome that another role does not permit, the roles can be distinguished by their rules rather than by first declaring two objects and comparing them.

The experiment therefore shifts distinction from object-level comparison to **difference in constructional specification**.

## 3. Result

A meaningful distinction between roles can be described without using the numeral “two” or a pre-existing collection, provided the roles have different constructional conditions. However, this does not yet establish multiplicity in the strong sense. We have shown only that there can be **non-identical constructional specifications** or alternative roles.

A further dependency appears: saying that two specifications differ presupposes a criterion for comparing specifications. This comparison can itself be structural (different admissibility conditions) rather than numerical, but the notion of difference must be formalized.

Therefore the experiment supports a weak pre-numerical notion of **difference/alternative**, but not yet a cardinal notion of multiplicity.

## 4. Decision

Accept **structural distinguishability of constructional roles** as a provisional pre-numerical candidate. Do not infer “two”, cardinality, or a collection from it.

The next experiment should test whether a single role can be accompanied by a distinct alternative within one constructional context, and what minimal structure is needed to speak of coexistence without importing a set or number.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Provisional`

## 6. Next Cycle

Test minimal coexistence of distinguishable constructional alternatives in one context, while avoiding set membership, cardinal numbers, and classical bivalence.
