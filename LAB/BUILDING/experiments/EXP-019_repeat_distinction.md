# EXP-019 — Repeating the Distinction-Making Act

**Brick / context:** `B-001`, `B-002`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether the primitive distinction-making capability `δ` can be applied again so as to produce another referable constructional result, without assuming in advance that the result is distinct from the first.

## 2. Experiment

Reapply the chosen distinction-making act in the same constructional context. Introduce a second reference label `s` only as a candidate for a newly produced result.

```text
δ → r
δ → s ?
```

The question is whether the second application itself justifies treating `s` as a result distinct from `r`.

## 3. Result

The experiment shows that **repeatability of the constructional act is not sufficient to establish multiplicity**. We can perform another distinction-making act, but the current rules provide no criterion that forces its candidate result `s` to be different from `r`.

Thus two acts have been described, but this still does not amount to two distinguishable objects. The distinction between acts and the distinction between their outcomes must not be conflated.

This exposes a precise missing ingredient: if the construction is to generate multiple referable results, we need a rule connecting repeated distinction acts to distinguishable outputs, or an additional primitive that supplies such a criterion.

## 4. Decision

Do not create `B-004` as a second object/result. Record the failure as a structural constraint:

> Repetition of a distinction-making act does not by itself generate distinguishable multiplicity.

The next experiment should therefore test the weakest explicit choice that could connect distinct constructional acts with distinct referable outputs, while keeping that choice clearly marked as an added primitive rather than a discovered necessity.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Accepted negative result / provisional constraint`

## 6. Next Cycle

Test an explicit act-identity or occurrence distinction: can constructional acts themselves be made distinguishable, and does that provide a legitimate basis for distinguishing their outputs?
