# EXP-004 — Repeatable Recognition

**Brick / context:** `B-001`  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether repeatable recognition can be specified without presupposing numerical repetition or a fully primitive equality relation.

## 2. Experiment

Treat recognition operationally: after a construction produces a result, a later reference or re-performance of the same construction may be checked against the earlier result. The experiment asks whether this can be expressed without saying that there are already two numerically distinct objects or that they are equal by a primitive equality predicate.

## 3. Result

The attempt exposes a dependency rather than eliminating one. To say that a later result is “recognized as the same” requires some criterion of sameness. If that criterion is merely stipulated, the construction has hidden equality inside recognition. If it is derived from the construction itself, we need a rule specifying what counts as the same construction/result.

However, an important distinction emerges: **recognition need not initially mean a metaphysical identity claim**. It can be treated as a rule of admissible re-reference or re-identification. This allows us to postpone full equality while making the dependency explicit.

## 4. Decision

Do not promote repeatable recognition to a primitive object-level equality relation. Retain it as a **constructional rule** whose formal content must be specified. The next experiment should test whether the rule can be based on the reproducibility/structure of a construction rather than on pre-existing equality between outputs.

## 5. Epistemic Status

`Provisional`

## 6. Next Cycle

Test construction reproducibility: whether performing a specified construction again can yield a re-identifiable result by virtue of the construction rule itself, without assuming numerical repetition or primitive equality.
