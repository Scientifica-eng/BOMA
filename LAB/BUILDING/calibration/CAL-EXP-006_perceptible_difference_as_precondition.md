# CAL-EXP-006 — Perceptible Difference as Precondition of Distinction

**Cycle:** `CAL-001`  
**Scope:** B-001 and the primitive starting point  
**Status:** Provisional result

## 1. Intention / Goal

Refine the starting point of the construction by distinguishing the condition that makes a distinction possible from the act of distinguishing itself.

## 2. Experiment

Test the following ordering:

```text
perceptible/registerable difference
            ↓
     possibility of distinction
            ↓
        distinction δ
            ↓
     referable result r
```

The purpose is not to claim that a metaphysical difference has been derived, but to identify the minimum condition under which a distinction can occur within the constructional protocol.

## 3. Result

The experiment supports treating δ as a secondary constructive act rather than the absolute primitive.

A distinction cannot be operationally performed unless there is some difference that can, in the broadest sense, be registered. This does not require two numerically individuated objects, nor a prior cardinality notion.

A suitable provisional formulation is:

> **A perceptible/registerable difference is a condition of possibility for the act of distinction.**

The term "perceptible" must be understood broadly: it does not commit the project to human consciousness. It means that the difference can in principle be registered by the mechanism through which the construction is being performed.

## 4. Important Boundary

This does not establish that "difference" is ontologically prior in an absolute metaphysical sense. It establishes only an operational dependency:

```text
no registerable difference
        ↓
no distinguishable constructional outcome
```

The project therefore avoids both extremes:

- `δ creates difference from nothing` — too strong;
- `difference is a fully specified metaphysical primitive` — also too strong.

## 5. Consequences for B-007

The earlier numerical-risk analysis of "exactly one" should not be repaired by calling the extension event merely "one perceptual event". Event unity itself must not be silently identified with the natural number 1.

The safer formulation remains that the extension protocol is **stipulated to have a designated new output-role**. Its uniqueness belongs to the chosen protocol, not to a numerical measurement.

Thus CAL-EXP-006 refines B-001 but does not by itself solve B-007.

## 6. Consequences for B-013/B-014

Likewise, replacing equality of depth by "absence of a perceptible difference in the number of events" would still risk smuggling counting into the construction.

The preferred repair remains structural equivalence of histories:

```text
H₁ ≃ H₂
```

where equivalence is determined by preserved constructional relations rather than by counting events.

## 7. Decision

1. Refine the conceptual starting point from `δ` alone to a two-level distinction:
   - condition: perceptible/registerable difference;
   - act: distinction `δ`.
2. Retain `δ` as the first explicit constructive operation.
3. Do not claim a metaphysically absolute primitive.
4. Do not use "one event" as a numerical justification anywhere in the construction.
5. Preserve the previous B-001 wording as historical evidence; the refined formulation should govern the repaired path.

## 8. Calibration Finding

The project now has a more honest hierarchy:

```text
difference capable of being registered
        ↓
possibility of distinguishing
        ↓
distinction δ
        ↓
referable result r
```

This is a methodological improvement because it makes explicit what was previously hidden in the phrase "perform a distinction".

## 9. Relation to the External Critique

The critique suggesting that figure/ground discrimination can occur without two same-kind objects is compatible with this result. The construction does not require two numerically individuated objects. It requires only a difference capable of being registered within the chosen context.

The appeal to Spencer-Brown is treated as philosophical motivation and comparison, not as an imported formal foundation.

## 10. Next Step

Close `CAL-001` with a cycle-level audit and decide whether the repaired starting point should be represented by a new Brick or by a revision of B-001. The decision must preserve the historical record of the original experiment.
