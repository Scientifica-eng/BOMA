# REVERSE-002 — Decomposition of Initiality, Successor, and Closure

**Cycle:** `REVERSE-001`  
**Status:** Provisional result

## 1. Intention / Goal

Decompose the three candidate ingredients of natural-number structure — initiality, successor-like transition, and closure/induction — and determine which are genuinely distinct structural requirements and which can be derived or represented through others.

## 2. Initiality

A natural-number-like structure requires a distinguished starting point from which generation begins.

The role is not numerical by itself. It can be described structurally as:

> there is a designated stage with no predecessor generated by the successor-like transition within the intended structure.

Initiality therefore appears to be a genuine structural role rather than merely a notation for zero.

## 3. Successor-like Transition

The structure requires a rule that takes a stage to a designated next stage.

A local rule of this form is not sufficient to characterize `N`, because truncated chains also possess such a rule.

The essential features still need separation:

- existence of a next stage;
- preservation of prior structural information, if required by the chosen model;
- distinction between the next stage and the predecessor, if required;
- compatibility with the intended generation principle.

Thus "successor" is better understood initially as a structural transition role, not as an already numerical function.

## 4. Closure / Induction

The reverse experiment confirms that local initiality plus successor do not exclude truncated or non-generated structures.

A third condition is therefore required. There are several equivalent-looking ways to state it, depending on the foundational framework:

- closure under the successor operation together with a generation/minimality condition;
- induction over all substructures closed under successor and containing the initial point;
- recursive generation from the initial point.

These formulations should not be assumed identical without proof. They may encode the same structural content under additional background assumptions, but that issue belongs to formalization rather than the current pre-mathematical stage.

## 5. Decomposition Finding

The target structure is not adequately represented as three independent "ingredients" of equal status.

A better conceptual decomposition is:

```text
initial point
      +
successor-like generation rule
      +
principle excluding unintended extra stages / enforcing generated closure
```

The third item is not simply another local operation. It is a **global structural constraint** on the domain generated by the first two.

This distinction matters for the forward path: trying to create closure as another Brick of the same local kind as `E` may be conceptually mistaken.

## 6. What Can Be Stripped Away?

### Numerical labels

Can be removed entirely. The structure can be described without `0,1,2,...`.

### Arithmetic operations

Addition and multiplication are not required to state the core natural-number structure. They can be treated as later constructions or derived recursive operations.

### Set-theoretic representation

The use of a set as a container is a formal representation choice. The natural-number structure can be described structurally without committing to a specific set-theoretic encoding.

### Order notation

A successor-generated relation may induce a canonical order later, but the order relation itself need not be primitive at the start.

## 7. What Appears Irreducible So Far?

The reverse analysis currently leaves three roles that cannot yet be eliminated:

1. a distinguished initial stage;
2. a successor-like transition rule;
3. a global generation/closure condition.

However, these are still roles in a mathematical characterization, not yet candidates for pre-mathematical primitives.

## 8. Provisional Conclusion

The reverse path suggests that the true target is not simply:

```text
initial + successor
```

but:

```text
initial + generative transition + generatedness/closure
```

This is a significant refinement because it tells the forward construction what it must eventually account for.

## 9. Next Reverse Experiment

Decompose each of the three roles further:

- What is the weakest notion of "initial" that does not presuppose predecessor arithmetic?
- What is the weakest notion of a generative transition that does not presuppose successor numbers?
- Can generatedness/closure be expressed as a constructional process rather than as a ready-made induction axiom?

The last question is especially important: if it cannot, the project may need to acknowledge induction/closure as an explicit foundational choice rather than pretending to derive it from a purely local construction.
