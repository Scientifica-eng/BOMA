# CLOSURE-CORE-008 — Minimal Objectification

## Purpose
Test the weakest commitment needed to turn the finite construction horizon into a reusable mathematical reference, without yet declaring a total mathematical domain.

## Working distinction
A stable reference to a construction pattern is weaker than declaring a completed domain containing all such patterns.

We therefore distinguish:

1. **Generated behavior** — what the protocol actually produces.
2. **Reusable pattern reference** — a stable referent for a generated structural pattern, independent of incidental provenance.
3. **Formal totality** — an explicit commitment that there is one completed mathematical domain containing the admissible patterns.

## Experiment
We tested whether a finite construction pattern can be promoted to a reusable referent while retaining only the structural behavior needed for later comparison and composition.

The minimal candidate is:

> A generated finite pattern may be treated as a stable referent for subsequent operations, while no claim is yet made that the collection of all such referents forms a completed domain.

## Result
The experiment is coherent at the operational level.

A reusable referent can be introduced without immediately importing:

- set membership;
- cardinality;
- natural-number notation;
- Peano axioms;
- a completed totality.

However, the act of declaring a stable referent is itself an explicit abstraction commitment. It is therefore **not derived for free** from the raw construction history.

## Boundary
The remaining distinction is now:

```text
finite generated pattern
        ↓
stable reusable referent
        ↓
[formal totality commitment]
        ↓
completed mathematical domain
```

The first arrow is weaker than the second.

## Consequence
The project should not describe stable objectification as equivalent to constructing the natural numbers. It is a controlled intermediate commitment that may allow more behavior to be studied before the final totality choice.

## Status
**Experimental result:** coherent intermediate abstraction; not yet a proof of mathematical totality.
