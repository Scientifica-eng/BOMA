# CLOSURE-CORE-005 — Neutral Unification of the Finite Horizon

## Status
Confirmed experimental result.

## Stage
Plan stage 7 — Closure / Completeness.

## Question
Can all permitted finite construction behaviors be referred to through one neutral constructional horizon, without treating that horizon as a completed mathematical totality?

## Setup
Start from the finite-behavior layer already established:

- a distinguished initial role;
- one elementary extension role;
- finite construction histories;
- composition of histories;
- construction depth;
- finite behavioral completeness.

No natural numbers, cardinality, set membership, induction schema, or named mathematical framework is introduced.

## Test
We distinguish three claims:

1. Every permitted finite history has a recoverable place in the construction protocol.
2. All such finite histories may be discussed relative to one common procedural horizon.
3. That common horizon is itself a completed mathematical object.

## Result
Claims (1) and (2) can be maintained together at the procedural level.

A single neutral reference to the finite construction horizon can be used as shorthand for the family of all permitted finite behaviors, provided that the reference is understood operationally rather than as a completed domain.

Claim (3) does not follow automatically. Turning the neutral horizon into a completed mathematical object introduces an additional existence/formation commitment.

## Key distinction

Operational unification:

```text
all permitted finite histories
        ↓
one common constructional horizon
```

Formal totality:

```text
common constructional horizon
        ↓
completed mathematical entity containing all finite stages
```

The first is compatible with theoretical neutrality. The second is the unresolved foundational step.

## Consequence
We can postpone commitment to a completed totality while retaining a unified account of all finite behaviors. This lowers, but does not eliminate, the remaining foundational commitment.

## Next test
Determine whether the neutral horizon can support a stable notion of depth comparison across all finite behaviors, without converting the horizon itself into a completed mathematical object.
