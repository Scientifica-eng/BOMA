# PHASE-9-004 — Framework-Neutral Characterization

## Status

Phase 9 — Formal matching against the natural-number target.

## Purpose

Attempt to state the common constructional core obtained before formalization strongly enough to support a framework-neutral characterization, while testing whether such a statement genuinely yields uniqueness or merely relocates framework assumptions into the specification.

## Proposed neutral specification

The target structure is described operationally by the following requirements:

1. A distinguished initial structural role is available.
2. There is one elementary extension/transition role.
3. Finite histories are generated by repeated elementary extension.
4. Finite histories admit decomposition by reversing elementary extensions.
5. Histories admit sequential composition.
6. Sequential composition behaves associatively.
7. Construction depth is invariant under the permitted identity of histories.
8. The empty history may serve as an identity when explicitly admitted.
9. Every finite construction remains locally referable and recoverable.

This specification deliberately avoids the terms set, natural number, equality, induction, monoid, freeness, initial algebra, and natural-number object.

## Test question

Does the neutral specification by itself determine one mathematical structure, up to isomorphism, strongly enough to identify the natural-number structure?

## Result

No. The specification is strong enough to describe the common finite behavior observed in the Peano-style and free-monoid matches, but it does not by itself force a unique formal ontology or a unique global completion principle.

In particular, the following remain open or framework-dependent:

- what counts as the formal domain containing the finite histories;
- what identity relation is used between formal objects;
- whether the empty history is promoted to a formal identity element;
- what principle identifies the intended totality of all finite constructions;
- what criterion makes the resulting structure canonical;
- which external notion of isomorphism is being used.

## Boundary finding

The neutral specification successfully identifies a **behavioral invariant core** shared by multiple formal interpretations. It does not, by itself, establish a framework-free uniqueness theorem.

The key distinction is:

```text
neutral behavioral characterization
        ≠
framework-neutral existence theorem
        ≠
framework-neutral uniqueness theorem
```

A uniqueness claim can only be made after an additional formal principle is supplied that turns the behavioral specification into a determinate mathematical object.

## Consequence

The project should not attempt to force the neutral specification to do more than it actually does. Its proper role is to act as a specification of the pre-formal behavior that candidate frameworks must realize.

The natural-number target can then be tested by asking, under each declared framework:

- whether the framework realizes the neutral specification;
- whether the resulting object is canonical within that framework;
- which added commitments are necessary;
- whether the resulting object is isomorphic to the intended natural-number structure.

## Current conclusion

The strongest framework-neutral claim currently supported is:

> The reverse-engineered construction specifies a robust finite successor-like behavioral core, but the passage from that core to a unique complete mathematical object requires an additional formal commitment.

This is not a failure of the construction. It identifies precisely where framework neutrality ends.
