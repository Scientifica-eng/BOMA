# CLOSURE-CORE-004 — Finite-Behavior Arithmetic Without Full Totality

## Status
Experimental / provisional.

## Question
Does completion of all finite construction behaviors suffice to recover the basic arithmetic behavior needed by the project, without postulating a completed totality of all stages?

## Working context
The project has established a distinction between:

- the operational horizon: behaviors actually constructed;
- the possible horizon: behaviors that can be continued by the protocol;
- a formally completed domain: a declared totality containing the relevant behaviors.

Earlier tests established that the second does not automatically provide the third.

## Experiment
We treat finite construction histories as admissible whenever they are generated by the established transition protocol. We then ask whether the operations already identified — extension, composition, and construction depth — can support the following behavior without introducing a completed domain:

1. extend a finite history by one elementary transition;
2. compose two finite histories;
3. recover the resulting finite construction depth;
4. compare finite construction depths by the project's already-defined structural recovery procedures.

No symbols for natural numbers, cardinality, set membership, induction, or a named algebraic structure are introduced.

## Observation
The finite behaviors can support a coherent arithmetic-like practice:

- one elementary extension changes the depth in a stable way;
- composition of finite histories yields another finite history;
- the depth of a composite history behaves compositionally with the depths of its components;
- the empty-history candidate acts as a boundary identity when that candidate is explicitly admitted.

Thus a substantial amount of arithmetic-like behavior can be recovered over finite construction histories without postulating a completed totality.

## Limitation
This does **not** establish a completed domain of all finite depths as a mathematical object.

It establishes only that every finite behavior encountered or generated by the protocol can participate in the relevant operations and that its depth can be structurally recovered.

The distinction is:

```text
all finite behaviors are individually admissible
        ≠
there exists a formally completed totality of all such behaviors
```

## Decision
The finite-behavior layer should be treated as a legitimate pre-formal arithmetic regime rather than being prematurely identified with the natural numbers.

The remaining foundational question is therefore narrower:

> What additional commitment, if any, is required to pass from individually admissible finite behaviors to a single formally completed domain that supports the project-wide arithmetic structure?
