# CLOSURE-CORE-002 — Minimal Completion Commitment

**Parent experiment:** CLOSURE-CORE-001  
**Status:** Provisional

## 1. Question

What is the weakest additional commitment needed to move from a generated, indefinitely continuable construction horizon to a completed mathematical domain, without importing the natural numbers or a specific mathematical foundation by name?

## 2. Distinctions

Three claims must remain separate:

1. **Indefinite continuability:** from every accepted stage, the construction rule permits another extension.
2. **Open-ended horizon:** there is no final stage forced by the protocol itself.
3. **Completed domain:** there is a formal totality whose elements include the stages relevant to the intended arithmetic structure.

The first does not entail the third. The second is weaker than the third.

## 3. Tested Minimal Commitments

### A. Mere repeatability

Rule: whenever a stage is available, another extension is available.

**Result:** insufficient. This gives ongoing possibility, not a completed totality.

### B. Existence of an indefinitely extendable carrier

Rule: there is a carrier supporting unlimited continuation in the transition role.

**Result:** stronger than A, but still does not specify a completed domain of all relevant stages.

### C. Acceptance of a completed horizon

Rule: the construction is admitted as having a total formal horizon containing the stages generated by the protocol.

**Result:** sufficient in direction, but this is an explicit foundational commitment. It is not derived from the local transition rule.

## 4. Key Finding

The weakest candidate we have identified is not yet a particular mathematical axiom, but a **completion commitment**:

> Admit a formal totality corresponding to the indefinitely extendable construction horizon.

This commitment is deliberately representation-neutral. It does not yet say whether the totality is a set, type, category, collection, class, or another formal object.

## 5. What Remains Unresolved

The phrase "formal totality corresponding to the horizon" is intentionally schematic. It may conceal substantial logical strength. In particular, the following remain open:

- whether such a totality can be justified internally;
- whether its existence already entails a numerical notion equivalent to the natural numbers;
- whether a weaker completion principle is possible;
- whether different foundational frameworks realize the same completion behavior with different primitive commitments.

## 6. Decision

Do **not** treat completion as derived. Treat it as the first explicit unresolved foundational commitment unless a later experiment produces an internal construction of the required totality.

The project therefore preserves the following boundary:

```text
pre-mathematical construction
        ↓
indefinite continuability
        ↓
open-ended horizon
===============================
completion commitment
===============================
formal mathematical totality
```

## 7. Next Test

Test whether the completion commitment can be weakened further by requiring only the preservation of all **finite construction behaviors** rather than asserting a completed totality directly.
