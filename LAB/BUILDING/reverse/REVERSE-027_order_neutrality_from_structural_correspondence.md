# REVERSE-027 — Order Neutrality from Structural Correspondence

**Status:** Experiment completed — provisional result

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether order-invariance of a pre-counting correspondence can be derived from a more primitive structural correspondence principle rather than introduced as a separate numerical rule.

## 2. Experiment

Consider an external differentiable plurality being processed against an unlabeled pattern chain.

Compare two processing orders:

```text
A = [a, b, c]
B = [c, a, b]
```

The positional assignments differ under a naive sequential procedure. We therefore impose a weaker requirement:

> the correspondence must preserve the structural pattern rather than the temporal order in which the external items are processed.

The relevant invariant is not a final numerical label, but preservation of the same structural correspondence type.

## 3. Result

A structural correspondence principle can explain why two different processing orders may yield the same pre-counting outcome **provided that** the correspondence rule is itself insensitive to presentation order and preserves the same structural relations.

However, this neutrality is not generated by the pattern chain alone.

The dependency is therefore:

```text
pattern chain
      ↓
correspondence rule
      ↓
structural preservation
      ↓
order-neutral outcome
```

The order-neutrality is consequently not yet a derived fact of the chain. It is a property of the chosen correspondence protocol.

## 4. What This Does Not Establish

The experiment does not establish:

- numerical equality of final outputs;
- cardinality;
- a formal equivalence relation;
- commutativity as a theorem of arithmetic;
- that the resulting invariant is already a natural number.

The word "same" remains operational and structural in this document, not formal numerical equality.

## 5. Critical Finding

The attempt to derive order-neutrality from structural correspondence reveals a boundary:

> **To obtain an order-independent pre-counting invariant, one must choose a correspondence protocol that preserves structure under reordering.**

Thus order-neutrality is not free. It is a declared structural requirement of the correspondence mechanism unless a deeper principle is later found that derives it.

## 6. Relation to the Construction

The current sequence is:

```text
unlabeled pattern chain
        ↓
external differentiable plurality
        ↓
structural correspondence
        ↓
order-neutral invariant
        ↓
pre-counting quantity candidate
```

This remains compatible with the project's theoretical neutrality: no known mathematical framework is required to state the experiment.

## 7. Decision

1. Retain structural correspondence as the main pre-counting mechanism.
2. Treat order-neutrality as an explicit protocol property, not yet as a derived theorem.
3. Do not call the invariant a number.
4. Do not import cardinality or arithmetic commutativity to justify the result.
5. Continue searching for a deeper reason why structure-preserving correspondence should be order-neutral.

## 8. Next Question

Can order-neutrality be derived from an even weaker principle such as **exchangeability of independent presentations** while preserving only the constructional distinction and correspondence structure?
