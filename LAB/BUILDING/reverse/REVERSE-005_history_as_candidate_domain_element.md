# REVERSE-005 — Construction Histories as Candidate Domain Elements

**Context:** `REVERSE-004`  
**Status:** Provisional result

## 1. Intention / Goal

Determine whether finite construction histories themselves can serve as the referable elements of the abstract domain, or whether an additional foundational choice is required to turn constructional histories into mathematical objects.

## 2. Experiment

Consider a concrete construction history:

```text
K → δ → r → E → r'
```

and its later extensions.

There are two possible readings:

### Reading A — History as operational record

The history is merely a record of what happened during construction. It is not yet an element of an abstract domain.

### Reading B — History as a domain element

The history is reified as a referable constructional object that can itself participate in later structural relations and operations.

The experiment asks whether Reading B follows automatically from the existence of Reading A.

## 3. Result

Reading B does **not** follow automatically.

A record of construction and an object belonging to a mathematical domain are conceptually different roles. The transition from:

```text
record of construction
```

to:

```text
domain element
```

requires an additional representational or foundational choice.

However, the reverse analysis also shows that such a choice can be made without immediately introducing numerical structure. One may stipulate that certain construction histories are admissible referable carriers of the generated structure.

This creates a clean boundary:

```text
pre-mathematical protocol
        ↓
recorded histories
        ↓  [explicit representational choice]
referable abstract carriers
        ↓
mathematical domain
```

## 4. Critical Finding

We have located a plausible **foundational choice point**.

The choice is not necessarily "choose the natural numbers". It is weaker:

> **Choose to treat structurally admissible construction histories (or their structural equivalence classes) as referable carriers of the generated domain.**

Once this choice is made, the project has crossed from a mere construction protocol into a candidate mathematical domain.

## 5. Relation to the Forward Path

This point corresponds closely to difficulties encountered in the forward path:

```text
forward:
construction history → ??? → abstract stage

reverse:
abstract domain element ← explicit reification of history
```

The convergence is significant. Both paths point toward the same missing operation: **reification of constructional history as a domain-level carrier**.

## 6. What This Does Not Establish

It does not establish that the carriers form a set in the set-theoretic sense.

It does not establish that the carriers are natural numbers.

It does not establish that all admissible histories form a completed infinite totality.

It does not establish an equivalence relation or quotient as a finished mathematical construction.

Those may be later choices or derived structures.

## 7. Decision

Accept the reification of structurally admissible construction histories as a **candidate foundational choice point**.

Do not yet adopt it as the project's final foundation.

Record the current boundary as:

```text
BEFORE THE CHOICE
constructional protocol + records

CHOICE POINT
reify admissible histories as domain carriers

AFTER THE CHOICE
candidate mathematical domain
```

## 8. Next Experiment

Test the weakest possible reification rule. In particular:

> What exactly makes a construction history admissible as a carrier, and can admissibility be defined without already assuming a completed set, numerical cardinality, or natural-number indexing?

This is now the immediate reverse-engineering target.
