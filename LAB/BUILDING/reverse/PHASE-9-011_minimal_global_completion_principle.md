# PHASE-9-011 — Minimal Global Completion Principle

## Status

Phase 9 — formal matching against the natural-number target.

## Purpose

Attempt to replace the two completion-sensitive clauses identified in PHASE-9-010 — generation completeness and indefinite extendability — with a single weaker global principle that can be stated without naming a specific mathematical framework.

## Candidate principle

The candidate is:

> **Global completion principle:** the intended domain contains exactly the stages that are finitely generated from the distinguished initial role by repeated elementary extension, and the extension operation remains available throughout that intended domain.

This is deliberately phrased behaviorally. It does not name a set, induction, a monoid, an initial algebra, a category, or a natural-number object.

## Compression result

The two clauses from PHASE-9-010 can be compressed conceptually into one statement about the intended completed domain:

```text
finite generation
+
continued applicability of extension
+
no extra admitted stages
```

However, this compression is not a proof-theoretic reduction. The candidate principle still contains both directions of the earlier burden:

- inclusion of all finitely generated stages;
- exclusion of non-generated stages;
- continued availability of the generating transition.

It is therefore a **single packaged commitment**, not demonstrably a weaker logical axiom in every framework.

## Cross-framework comparison

### Peano-style

The principle corresponds closely to specifying the generated successor domain and its closure/induction behavior. The packaging is framework-neutral only at the behavioral level; formal realization still uses a carrier, equality, successor, and induction/closure principle.

### Free monoid

The principle corresponds to restricting the formal carrier to the finite words generated by the distinguished generator and ensuring the composition/identity structure is available. Freeness or an equivalent universal property is still an additional formal commitment if canonicity is required.

### Initial algebra

The principle is naturally expressed through generated terms and the requirement that the intended carrier be the initial algebra for the chosen signature. Initiality remains an external mechanism for canonicity.

### Natural-number object

The principle corresponds behaviorally to the object generated from zero and successor, but the existence of the object and its universal property still require the categorical ambient setting.

## Main result

The two global completion conditions can be compressed into a single framework-neutral behavioral principle, but the experiment does **not** show that the resulting principle is logically weaker than the framework-specific closure mechanisms.

More importantly, the principle reveals the exact unavoidable global content:

```text
all and only finitely generated stages
+
continued extension within the intended totality
```

This is the core completion commitment that the finite construction itself did not supply.

## Foundational boundary

The project can therefore distinguish:

```text
Local pre-formal construction
        ↓
Finite generative behavior
        ↓
Global completion principle  ← irreducible burden currently identified
        ↓
Framework-specific formal realization
        ↓
Canonical mathematical object
```

The present evidence does not justify claiming that the global completion principle can be removed while preserving the intended target.

## Decision

Treat the candidate principle as the **minimal packaged completion statement currently identified**, not as a framework-free theorem.

Do not promote it to a primitive fact of the pre-formal layer.

## Remaining question

Can the global completion principle itself be derived from a single universal characterization of the target, or is its adoption an irreducible constitutive choice?

## Rule for Phase 9

A packaged principle that combines previously separate commitments must not be described as logically weaker unless an explicit relative-strength argument is available.
