# REVERSE-011 — Forward/Reverse Convergence at the Generated-Construction Horizon

**Status:** Provisional result
**Purpose:** Compare the current forward reconstruction path with the reverse-engineering path and determine whether they converge on the same pre-formal boundary.

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether the forward path from registerable difference and constructive operations and the reverse path from the natural-number structure converge on the same intermediate object before a formal mathematical domain is introduced.

## 2. Forward Path Snapshot

The repaired forward path currently tends toward:

```text
registerable difference
        ↓
constructive distinction
        ↓
constructional trace
        ↓
referability
        ↓
extension
        ↓
referable carriers
        ↓
organized generated horizon
```

The forward path deliberately avoids introducing a formal set/domain too early.

## 3. Reverse Path Snapshot

The reverse path currently tends toward:

```text
natural-number structure
        ↓
initiality + successor-like transition + closure/generation
        ↓
generated carriers
        ↓
referable organized constructional horizon
```

The reverse path also identifies a boundary where a formal mathematical domain is no longer forced by the generation protocol itself and may require an explicit foundational choice.

## 4. Comparison Experiment

Compare the outputs of the two paths at the strongest common level that does not yet assume a formal set-domain.

Candidate common description:

> **A generated, referable constructional horizon consisting of a distinguished initial carrier and carriers related by the extension protocol.**

The comparison ignores the concrete implementation details of the forward traces and the numerical labels or axiomatic packaging of the reverse target.

## 5. Result

The two paths converge substantially at this horizon.

The forward path reaches it by abstraction upward from concrete constructional events.

The reverse path reaches it by decomposition downward from the natural-number target.

The convergence is not a proof that this horizon is uniquely necessary. It is evidence that the same intermediate architecture is independently motivated from both directions.

The two paths still disagree on one unresolved issue:

> whether the generated horizon itself should be treated as a mathematical domain, or whether an explicit foundational step is required to turn the organized carriers into the elements of such a domain.

This disagreement is now sharply localized.

## 6. Critical Finding

The project has likely moved its true foundational choice upward from the original proposed starting point.

The most defensible architecture currently appears to be:

```text
CONTEXT / PRECONDITION
        ↓
CONSTRUCTIONAL PROTOCOL
        ↓
GENERATED, REFERABLE HORIZON
        ↓
[FOUNDATIONAL CHOICE / FORMALIZATION]
        ↓
MATHEMATICAL DOMAIN
        ↓
NATURAL-NUMBER CHARACTERIZATION
```

This is a major methodological result. The project no longer needs to force every ingredient of the eventual mathematical domain to emerge from the pre-mathematical protocol itself.

## 7. Decision

1. Accept the generated referable horizon as the current convergence point of the forward and reverse paths.
2. Do not yet identify this horizon with a set, the natural numbers, or any standard mathematical domain.
3. Treat the transition from the horizon to a formal domain as the leading candidate for the project's explicit foundational choice.
4. Preserve both paths as independent evidence.
5. The next experiment should isolate the minimum formalization required at that transition and determine whether set/domain structure, equality, or another foundational device is the minimal choice.

## 8. Epistemic Status

**Strong provisional convergence, unresolved foundational boundary.**
