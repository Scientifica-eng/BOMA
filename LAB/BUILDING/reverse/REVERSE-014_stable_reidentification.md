# REVERSE-014 — Stable Re-identification from Construction History

**Status:** Provisional result  
**Context:** `REVERSE-013`

## 1. Intention / Goal

Test whether a constructional history alone can supply stable re-identification of a generated carrier, without importing a primitive mathematical equality relation.

## 2. Experiment

Consider a carrier `c` generated by a construction history `H`.

We test three candidate mechanisms:

### A — Current address

Use a temporary reference that points to the carrier while the current construction state retains it.

This supports access but does not by itself establish a stable identity beyond the reference mechanism.

### B — Construction history

Use the recorded origin of the carrier: the carrier is re-identified by the preserved constructional history that produced it.

```text
H → c
H preserved → access to c
```

This works as an operational re-identification rule provided the construction history is preserved and unambiguous within the protocol.

### C — Abstract equality

Declare that two references are equal when they refer to the same carrier.

This would provide a formal identity relation, but it is an additional mathematical/logical commitment and therefore cannot be counted as derived at this stage.

## 3. Result

The construction history is sufficient for **operational re-identification** within the protocol. A carrier can be referred to again through the preserved origin/history without first postulating a general mathematical equality relation.

However, construction history does not by itself establish an **abstract identity relation** over all possible carriers. It only supplies a method for tracing a carrier back to its production event.

Thus two levels must be separated:

```text
operational re-identification
        ≠
abstract equality/identity
```

## 4. Consequence

This weakens the earlier hypothesis that stable re-identification itself must be the first formal mathematical commitment.

A pre-formal system can retain stable references operationally through provenance/history.

A general identity relation becomes necessary only when the project wants to compare carriers independently of their construction histories, or to quotient different histories into the same abstract carrier.

## 5. Decision

Do not promote mathematical equality/identity to the foundational boundary yet.

Retain the distinction between:

1. referability through preserved history;
2. formal identity as an abstract relation.

The likely boundary may therefore lie later, at the moment the construction deliberately **forgets provenance** and wants different histories to count as the same abstract carrier.

## 6. Next Experiment

Test whether provenance can be deliberately forgotten while preserving enough structure to obtain an abstract carrier. This will identify whether **abstraction/quotienting** rather than equality itself marks the first genuinely mathematical commitment.
