# CLOSURE-CORE-007 — Finite Horizon Without Totality

## Purpose
Test whether the finite construction horizon can be specified by generation and recoverability, without introducing a totality axiom or a prior mathematical domain.

## Experimental question
Can we speak about the finite horizon without saying "the set of all finite stages"?

## Construction-neutral formulation
A finite stage is admitted when it is generated by the permitted construction protocol and its finite construction history can be recovered from the initial carrier through elementary extensions.

The horizon is therefore described operationally by:

- generation by the protocol;
- finite recoverability to the initial state;
- preservation of the transition role;
- closure of each admitted finite history under its own finite continuation.

No set-membership, cardinality, natural-number indexing, or formal induction principle is introduced here.

## Result
This gives a coherent description of a **finite-generated horizon** without quantifying over a pre-existing totality. The notion is therefore weaker than asserting a completed mathematical domain.

However, the experiment does **not** produce a single completed object containing every possible finite history as elements. The phrase "all finite histories" can be operationally understood as a rule of admissibility, but turning that rule into one mathematical totality remains an additional commitment.

## Key distinction

```text
finite-generated horizon
        ≠
completed mathematical totality
```

The experiment lowers the foundational commitment again, but does not eliminate the final completion step.

## Consequence for the project
The remaining closure problem is now sharply localized:

> What is the weakest commitment that turns the operational admissibility rule for finite histories into a reusable mathematical object?

The project should not assume that this commitment must be set-theoretic. Its nature is deliberately left open for later external formalization.
