# EXTERNAL-INTERPRETATION-001 — Candidate Mathematical Frameworks

## Status

Phase 8 — External mathematical interpretation.

This document deliberately does **not** declare that the pre-formal construction is already \(\mathbb N\). It records external mathematical structures that can serve as interpretations of the structure obtained through the reverse-engineering path.

## Pre-formal structure being interpreted

The construction developed so far supplies, at minimum:

- a distinguished initial structural role;
- an elementary extension/transition role;
- finite histories generated by repeated elementary extension;
- composition of histories;
- operational associativity of sequential composition;
- a candidate empty history as identity, when explicitly admitted;
- construction depth as a structural invariant;
- finite decomposition into elementary extensions;
- local referability without a prior global mathematical domain.

These are observations from the construction. They are **not** yet statements that a set, equality relation, natural-number object, or induction principle exists.

## Candidate A — Additive monoid / free monoid on one generator

A standard external interpretation is the additive monoid of non-negative integers, with addition and zero. The natural numbers including zero form a free monoid on one generator in the usual algebraic sense: the generator can be taken to correspond to one elementary step, finite composition corresponds to addition, and the empty word corresponds to zero.

### Fit

Very strong for:

- history composition;
- associativity;
- an identity represented by the empty history;
- one elementary generator;
- construction depth as the number of elementary generator occurrences.

### What it adds

The monoid interpretation adds formal objects, an operation, an identity, and the universal/free characterization. Those are not supplied merely by observing finite construction histories.

### Important restriction

The phrase "free monoid on one generator" is an **external identification**, not a premise of the construction. The project must first establish the required structural properties and only then compare them with this known framework.

## Candidate B — Peano-style successor structure

A second external interpretation is a natural-number structure with an initial element and successor operation. Standard Peano-style presentations include an initial natural number, closure under successor, successor non-origin, injectivity of successor, and an induction principle; exact axiom presentations vary by first- versus second-order formulation and by choice of zero or one as the initial element.

### Fit

Very strong for:

- initial role;
- elementary extension/successor;
- predecessor/decomposition of finite stages;
- the need to distinguish finite generation from a complete natural-number domain.

### What it adds

The decisive addition is a global principle such as induction or an equivalent completeness condition. This is precisely the unresolved closure issue identified in CLOSURE-CORE-001 through CLOSURE-CORE-013.

## Candidate C — Initial algebra / inductive datatype interpretation

A third interpretation treats the structure as the initial algebra generated by one base constructor and one unary successor-like constructor. This is conceptually close to the project's construction because it separates local constructors from the global object they generate.

### Fit

Very strong for:

- base case plus extension;
- finite generation;
- recursive decomposition;
- structural recursion over generated objects;
- explaining why the construction has a canonical finite shape.

### What it adds

The formal notion of an initial algebra is itself mathematical infrastructure. The project should therefore treat this as a candidate interpretation rather than as a hidden premise.

## Candidate D — Category-theoretic natural-number object

A category-theoretic interpretation may represent the natural-number structure as an object equipped with a zero-like map and successor-like map satisfying an appropriate universal property.

### Fit

Strong for separating:

- the structure itself;
- the maps/operations acting on it;
- the universal property that identifies the intended object.

### What it adds

It introduces categorical infrastructure and a universal-property criterion. This is substantially more formal machinery than is needed to describe the construction itself.

## Comparative result

The current evidence does **not** select one framework uniquely.

| Candidate | Fit to observed construction | Main extra commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Additive/free monoid | Very strong | formal monoid + identity + freeness |
| Peano successor structure | Very strong | formal domain + induction/completeness |
| Initial algebra | Very strong | algebraic/category-theoretic initiality |
| Natural-number object | Strong | categorical infrastructure + universal property |

Therefore the correct Phase-8 conclusion is **underdetermination**:

> The pre-formal construction has enough structure to admit multiple mathematically respectable interpretations. It should not be declared to "be" \(\mathbb N\) merely because one interpretation is available.

## What Phase 8 must test next

The next question is not "which theory is true?" It is:

> **Which formal commitments are necessary and sufficient to map the observed pre-formal structure into each candidate interpretation?**

In particular we should compare the cost of the candidates along four dimensions:

1. identity of structural stages;
2. global closure/completeness;
3. composition/addition;
4. uniqueness/canonicity of the resulting interpretation.

Only after this comparison should the project decide whether one framework is preferred, whether several are equally valid, or whether the project has discovered a framework-neutral core common to them.

## Prohibited shortcut

The following inference is explicitly rejected:

> "The construction looks like the free monoid on one generator, therefore it is \(\mathbb N\)."

The permitted procedure is:

> construct → isolate structural properties → compare against external theories → record added commitments → identify the resulting formal structure.
