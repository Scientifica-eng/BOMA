# REVERSE-013 — Field-Like Formalization Boundary

**Path:** Reverse engineering  
**Status:** Provisional result

## 1. Intention / Goal

Determine whether the generated-and-referable horizon can be organized into a field-like domain without importing a pre-existing mathematical theory of sets or natural numbers, and identify the smallest extra commitment required for formalization.

## 2. Experiment

Start from the established constructional horizon:

```text
initial carrier → extension → next carrier → ...
```

Assume only that actually generated carriers are available for reference and that extension induces a local predecessor/successor-like relation.

Test what additional ingredients are needed to speak of a formal domain.

Candidate ingredients:

1. **Designation:** a rule saying which generated carriers count as admissible carriers of the formal structure.
2. **Re-identification:** a criterion for recognizing the same carrier across references.
3. **Collective packaging:** a way of speaking about the admissible carriers as one formal domain.

## 3. Result

The first ingredient can be supplied by the construction protocol itself: a carrier is admissible when it is generated according to the accepted protocol.

The second ingredient cannot be eliminated entirely. Mere reference to a construction history does not by itself provide a general mathematical identity relation; at some point a formal system must permit stable re-identification of carriers.

The third ingredient is not logically prior in every possible formalization. The project can first speak operationally about a class of admissible carriers and their relations, and only later choose a set-theoretic or other formal representation.

Thus the boundary appears to be **re-identification/formal identity**, not necessarily set membership.

## 4. Key Finding

The current reverse path suggests:

```text
constructional generation
        ↓
admissible carriers
        ↓
stable re-identification
        ↓
formal domain
```

The move from admissible carriers to a formal domain therefore requires an explicit commitment about identity/re-identification, but does not force the project to choose set theory as the foundational language.

## 5. Methodological Consequence

This supports the new neutrality principle:

> A known mathematical theory may be used later as a formal representation or comparison framework, but the construction experiment need not adopt that theory merely to make the generated horizon intelligible.

The project can remain theory-neutral while still making its necessary commitments explicit.

## 6. Decision

1. Treat the generated horizon as a valid pre-formal structure of admissible carriers and transition relations.
2. Treat stable re-identification as the next unavoidable formal commitment.
3. Do not introduce set membership or natural-number labels at this stage.
4. Keep set theory, Peano arithmetic, type theory, category theory, and other frameworks as external comparison/representation options unless one is explicitly selected.

## 7. Open Question

Can stable re-identification itself be reconstructed from the constructional history and provenance mechanism, or must it be a declared primitive? This is now the most important unresolved question on the boundary.
