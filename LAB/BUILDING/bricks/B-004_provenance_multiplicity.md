# Brick B-004 — Provenance-Indexed Referable Multiplicity

**Status:** Provisional
**Type:** Chosen constructional rule
**Previous Bricks:** `B-001`, `B-002`
**Experiment:** `EXP-021`

## Definition

We adopt the convention that each separately recorded occurrence of the distinction-making act `δ` receives its own referable output token.

```text
δ₁ → r₁
δ₂ → r₂
```

The distinction between `r₁` and `r₂` is presently grounded in their separate constructional provenance.

## Why this Brick was chosen

`EXP-018` and `EXP-019` showed that neither applying a construction nor repeating `δ` automatically gives distinguishable outputs. `EXP-020` showed that occurrences can be separately recorded, but that this alone does not distinguish their outputs.

`B-004` therefore makes the missing connection explicit.

This is a **choice**, not a deduction.

## Current commitments

1. Separately recorded occurrences of `δ` may be assigned separately referable output tokens.
2. The provenance of an output is part of its current identity within the construction.
3. No numerical cardinality is assigned.
4. No set or pair is introduced.
5. No general classical identity/inequality principle is assumed.
6. The rule may later prove too strong, too weak, or unnecessary and may be revised.

## What the Brick gives us

We now have a minimal controlled multiplicity:

```text
r₁     r₂
│       │
δ₁      δ₂
```

This is **pre-numerical multiplicity**, not yet the number 2.

## What remains open

We must determine whether these tokens can be organized, compared, selected, reproduced, or otherwise operated on without silently introducing set theory or natural-number concepts.

In particular, we need to test whether multiplicity can be extended from two recorded outputs to an unbounded constructional process.

## Epistemic status

**Provisional / chosen rule.**

The Brick records a deliberate constructional decision and its current consequences; it is not a theorem or claim of foundational necessity.
